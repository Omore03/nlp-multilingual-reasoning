\begin{abstract}
We examine demonstration selection and prompt language in Urdu--English mathematical reasoning using few-shot in-context learning without updating model weights. The fixed development set contains ten GSM8K training questions and an approved pool of 18 bilingual solved demonstrations. We compare random and semantic-similarity selection under four language configurations, with hosted Gemma 4 31B and local quantized Gemma 3 4B and 1B models. Additional local zero-shot and random-seed checks yield seven completed runs and 380 scored configurations, all reusing the same ten questions. The main two-shot runs score 80/80, 64/80, and 16/80, respectively. A documented parser correction recovers 15 numerically correct answers without additional inference. The 4B model avoids the observed ceiling and Urdu floor, but its selection differences are not statistically established. We report reproducible evidence, output-limit failures and limitations; no accuracy improvement is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Worked demonstrations motivate chain-of-thought prompting~\cite{wei}, while multilingual reasoning research motivates comparing equivalent problems across languages~\cite{shi}. Semantic retrieval can select informative in-context examples instead of random ones~\cite{liu}. Our question is how Urdu example selection interacts with the language of questions, demonstrations and instructions. The proposed contribution is a controlled Urdu--English study and error analysis; the existing selection algorithms are not claimed as new inventions.

This follow-up baseline analysis is dated 25 November 2026 and presents the work completed for the intermediate deliverable. It extends the original hosted pilot with saved local-model runs, zero-shot controls and two additional random selection seeds. Baseline implementation is distinct from demonstrating a beneficial selection effect.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Frozen data and selection}
Hussain prepared 20 bilingual GSM8K training demonstrations and ten separate development questions~\cite{cobbe}. Recorded review approves 28 items; demonstration items 4 and 5 remain excluded for Urdu wording corrections. The resulting 18-item pool and ten development IDs are disjoint. The pinned original English training file confirms all 30 numerical keys; 25 questions match exactly and five differ only in whitespace. Review evidence is retained separately from automated arithmetic and structure checks. All 18 approved Urdu solutions contain English unit words, so the Urdu condition is nominal rather than strictly monolingual. Frozen text is not silently edited.

Random selection samples two distinct candidates using sorted IDs and a per-query seed derived from the base selection seed and question ID. Similarity ranks normalized Urdu questions by cosine similarity using \texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-}\allowbreak\texttt{mpnet-base-v2}~\cite{encoder}, revision \texttt{4328cf26...}. Thirty cached 768-dimensional vectors cover the original pool and development set; rankings use six-decimal scores and ID tie-breaks. The approved selections are frozen with hashes. Retrieval always uses Urdu, including the English prompt condition. Target answers and worked solutions are not supplied to the selector or inference prompt.

\subsection{Prompts and models}
Condition order is question/demonstrations/instructions: U-U-U, U-U-E, U-E-E and E-E-E, where U and E denote Urdu and English. Each question/strategy uses the same two ordered demonstrations across conditions. All prompts request working in the target language and a terminal \texttt{FINAL\_ANSWER:} number. Zero-shot removes demonstrations and their instruction; U-U-E and U-E-E then coincide, leaving three unique conditions and 30 calls per model.

The hosted model is \texttt{gemma-4-31b-it} through the free Google API. Local models are \texttt{gemma3:4b} and \texttt{gemma3:1b}, Q4\_K\_M, through Ollama 0.40.18 on Rayyan's Apple M5 laptop~\cite{models}. Full local digests are retained (prefixes \texttt{68ceab7e} and \texttt{97558784}). Shared numerical settings are temperature 0, top-$p$ 1, generation seed 42 and 2048 maximum output tokens. Local context is 4096; hosted inference uses minimal thinking. Model generation, backend, quantization and options differ, preventing a size-only causal interpretation. Local runs were verified from saved journals rather than repeated on the integration machine.

Main two-shot arms each contain $10\times2\times4=80$ configurations. The 4B random checks use selection seeds 43 and 44, each with 40 configurations; generation seed stays 42. Both local zero-shot runs contain 30. The seven completed runs therefore total 380 responses on ten distinct questions. The attempted hosted zero-shot reference returned repeated HTTP503 capacity failures and was stopped, leaving 0/30 successful responses; it is excluded from accuracy comparisons. No paid fallback is used.

\subsection{Evaluation and proposed extensions}
Numerical exact match normalizes Western, Urdu and Arabic-Indic digits and compares decimal values. The adopted parser prioritizes the last usable literal \texttt{FINAL\_ANSWER} segment, with optional colon; otherwise the earlier marker-agreement rule applies. Different numbers within a segment remain ambiguous. Local boundary hardening rejects markers embedded in other identifiers and leaves all archived predictions unchanged. Parser version, source hash and output hash are recorded in new evaluations. Both original score versions and raw responses remain preserved.

Completed local outputs reaching the token cap are scored as returned, while transport failures remain separate. Missing or ambiguous final answers are incorrect. Model-provided token totals remain below 4096 in the saved local runs; this saturation check is not an independent proof that no input truncation occurred. We compare strategies on matched question IDs with descriptive exact two-sided McNemar tests and report per-cell Wilson intervals in the supporting analysis. Repeated configurations are not independent questions.

The planned extensions are query-anchored diversity and reasoning-complexity selection~\cite{fu}, four-shot prompting and a separate reviewed MGSM evaluation. They remain outside this baseline implementation. A larger study must freeze its sampling and analysis before collecting results.

\subsection{Selection, language and parser effects}
The 31B arm reaches a ceiling: all eight cells score 10/10. The 1B arm scores 16/80 overall and 0/20 in nominal U-U-U. The 4B arm scores random 31/40 and similarity 33/40. In U-U-U the paired difference is 9/10 versus 6/10, with three similarity-only successes and none random-only (unadjusted $p=0.25$). Random seeds 42, 43 and 44 score 31, 30 and 32 out of 40; their U-U-U scores are 6, 6 and 9. No strategy advantage is established. Even 10/10 has a per-cell 95\% Wilson interval of approximately 72--100\%.

For 4B, zero-shot scores 8/10, 7/10 and 8/10 in the three unique conditions. Corresponding random two-shot scores are 6/10, 8/10 and 9/10; similarity scores 9/10, 7/10 and 9/10. The extra U-E-E two-shot condition shares the U-U-E zero-shot control. These matched results do not establish that demonstrations mainly teach formatting. English-minus-Urdu differences also vary with selection seed.

Offline rescoring reproduces 15 newly correct 4B answers, with zero lost: ten follow generic restatement markers and five omit the colon. Seven additional outputs become parsed wrong numbers. Before/after correct totals are 61 to 64 (4B main), 14 to 23 (4B zero-shot), 29 to 30 (seed 43) and 30 to 32 (seed 44). The 31B and 1B totals are unchanged. These gains correct measurement; they are not model improvements. A numerically correct soda answer reverses spent/remaining money in its prose, illustrating that exact match does not certify reasoning.

\subsection{Error analysis and limitations}
The 4B main run has 15 parsed wrong answers and one output-limit failure; 1B has 49 parsed wrong answers, 14 output-limit failures and one other parse failure. The 32-row draft worksheet covers all 16 noncorrect 4B main answers and two selected errors per 1B strategy/condition cell. Examples include treating an unavailable hotel floor as one room, counting three pies as three people, and omitting the teacher when counting eaters. The error tags remain draft annotations; no demonstration-copying cause or translation-quality certification is inferred from numerical errors alone.

All 380 records pass frozen-plan, data, prompt, identity and completeness checks. The offline suite passes 285 tests; all 14 configurations preview without inference, and an 80-record mock run and no-op resume pass. These are engineering checks rather than research outcomes. Main limitations are ten development questions, possible exposure to public training items, mixed-language units and model/runtime confounding. The original report and archived journals remain unchanged. Gemma 3 4B provides the main selection comparison, with the other models retained as reference arms.
