\begin{abstract}
We examine demonstration selection and prompt language in Urdu--English mathematical reasoning using few-shot in-context learning without updating model weights. The fixed development set contains ten GSM8K training questions and an approved pool of 18 bilingual solved demonstrations. We compare random and semantic-similarity selection under four language configurations, with hosted Gemma 4 31B and local quantized Gemma 3 4B and 1B models. Additional local zero-shot and random-seed checks yield seven completed runs and 380 scored configurations, all reusing the same ten questions. The main two-shot runs score 80/80, 64/80, and 16/80, respectively. A documented parser correction recovers 15 numerically correct answers without additional inference. The 4B model avoids the observed ceiling and Urdu floor, but its selection differences are not statistically established. Our next step is to select or construct a stronger reviewed dataset and repeat baseline analysis before implementing the proposed selection extensions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Worked demonstrations motivate chain-of-thought prompting~\cite{wei}, while multilingual reasoning research motivates comparing equivalent problems across languages~\cite{shi}. Semantic retrieval can select informative in-context examples instead of random ones~\cite{liu}. Our question is how Urdu example selection interacts with the language of questions, demonstrations and instructions. The proposed contribution is a controlled Urdu--English study and error analysis; the existing selection algorithms are not claimed as new inventions.

This intermediate analysis, dated 25 November 2026, extends the hosted pilot with local-model runs, zero-shot controls and random-seed checks. The next stage prioritizes better data before proposed method extensions.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Frozen data and selection}
Hussain supplied the bilingual GSM8K training data~\cite{cobbe}. The pilot uses 18 approved solved demonstrations and ten separate reviewed development questions with disjoint IDs. English questions and numerical keys were checked against the pinned original training file. Recorded review evidence is retained separately from automated arithmetic and structure checks. All 18 approved Urdu solutions contain English unit words, so the Urdu condition is nominal rather than strictly monolingual. The archived pilot data remain fixed.

Random selection samples two distinct candidates using a reproducible per-query seed. Similarity ranks normalized Urdu questions by cosine similarity using \texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-}\allowbreak\texttt{mpnet-base-v2}~\cite{encoder}, revision \texttt{4328cf26...}; six-decimal scores and ID tie-breaks fix ordering. Genuine cached vectors reproduce the frozen selections. Retrieval always uses Urdu, including the English prompt condition. Target answers and solutions are excluded from selection and prompting.

\subsection{Prompts and models}
Condition order is question/demonstrations/instructions: U-U-U, U-U-E, U-E-E and E-E-E, where U and E denote Urdu and English. Each question/strategy uses the same two ordered demonstrations across conditions. All prompts request working in the target language and a terminal \texttt{FINAL\_ANSWER:} number. Zero-shot removes demonstrations and their instruction; U-U-E and U-E-E then coincide, leaving three unique conditions and 30 calls per model.

The hosted model is \texttt{gemma-4-31b-it} through the free Google API. Local models are \texttt{gemma3:4b} and \texttt{gemma3:1b}, Q4\_K\_M, through Ollama 0.40.18~\cite{models}. Model digests and original runtime evidence are retained. Shared settings are temperature 0, top-$p$ 1, generation seed 42 and 2048 output tokens. Local context is 4096; hosted inference uses minimal thinking. Model generation, backend, quantization and options differ, preventing a size-only causal interpretation.

Main two-shot arms each contain $10\times2\times4=80$ configurations. The 4B random checks use selection seeds 43 and 44 (40 configurations each); generation seed stays 42. Local zero-shot arms add 30 each, yielding 380 completed responses on ten distinct questions. Hosted zero-shot stopped after repeated HTTP503 failures with 0/30 successful responses and is excluded from comparisons. No paid fallback is used.

\subsection{Evaluation}
Numerical exact match normalizes Western, Urdu and Arabic-Indic digits. The parser prioritizes the last usable literal \texttt{FINAL\_ANSWER} segment, with optional colon; otherwise the earlier marker-agreement rule applies. Different numbers within a segment remain ambiguous. Marker boundaries, parser versions and hashes are recorded; original score versions and raw responses are preserved.

Completed local cutoff outputs are scored as returned; transport failures remain separate. Missing or ambiguous answers are incorrect. Returned token totals stay below 4096, but do not independently prove absence of input truncation. Comparisons use matched questions and descriptive exact two-sided McNemar tests; supporting analysis includes per-cell Wilson intervals. Repeated configurations are not independent questions.

\subsection{Future plan and next steps}
\textbf{First, improve the data foundation.} Before starting the proposed novelty or further method improvements, we will assess existing English--Urdu mathematical-reasoning datasets and, if none is suitable, construct a curated dataset or extend a source. Criteria include faithful translations, consistent quantities/units, correct keys and worked solutions, and varied operations and reasoning difficulties. Human review and arithmetic checks will be recorded. Working targets are approximately 240 demonstration candidates and 40 development questions. Candidate, development and final-evaluation items will remain disjoint, with inclusion rules and versions fixed before comparative runs. The current pilot stays separate.

\textbf{Next, repeat baseline analysis on the improved data.} We will compare zero-shot, random and semantic-similarity baselines using matched questions, fixed model settings and multiple random selection seeds. Analysis will cover accuracy by language, operation and difficulty, plus numerical, interpretation, language and format errors. This data-and-baseline phase comes before novelty implementation; items will not be selected to favour a strategy.

\textbf{Then, implement and evaluate the proposed extensions.} We will implement query-anchored diversity and reasoning-complexity selection~\cite{fu}, compare two-shot and four-shot prompting, and study their interaction with Urdu/English prompt language. This controlled comparison is the proposed contribution; improvement must be measured. Finally, the approach will be frozen before separate paired English/Urdu MGSM evaluation, subject to translation-quality and usage checks. These are planned extensions, not completed results.

\subsection{Selection, language and parser effects}
The 31B arm reaches a ceiling: all eight cells score 10/10. The 1B arm scores 16/80 overall and 0/20 in nominal U-U-U. The 4B arm scores random 31/40 and similarity 33/40. In U-U-U the paired difference is 9/10 versus 6/10, with three similarity-only successes and none random-only (unadjusted $p=0.25$). Random seeds 42, 43 and 44 score 31, 30 and 32 out of 40; their U-U-U scores are 6, 6 and 9. No strategy advantage is established. Even 10/10 has a per-cell 95\% Wilson interval of approximately 72--100\%.

For 4B, zero-shot scores 8/10, 7/10 and 8/10 in the three unique conditions. Corresponding random two-shot scores are 6/10, 8/10 and 9/10; similarity scores 9/10, 7/10 and 9/10. The extra U-E-E two-shot condition shares the U-U-E zero-shot control. These matched results do not establish that demonstrations mainly teach formatting. English-minus-Urdu differences also vary with selection seed.

Offline rescoring recovers 15 correct 4B answers (ten generic-restatement and five missing-colon cases), loses none, and parses seven additional wrong answers. Correct totals change from 61 to 64 (4B main), 14 to 23 (zero-shot), 29 to 30 (seed 43) and 30 to 32 (seed 44); 31B and 1B are unchanged. These are measurement corrections, not model improvements. Numerical matching does not certify reasoning.

\subsection{Error analysis and limitations}
The 4B main run has 15 parsed wrong answers and one output-limit failure; 1B has 49 parsed wrong answers, 14 output-limit failures and one other parse failure. The 32-row draft worksheet covers all 16 noncorrect 4B main answers and two selected errors per 1B strategy/condition cell. Examples include treating an unavailable hotel floor as one room, counting three pies as three people, and omitting the teacher when counting eaters. The error tags remain draft annotations; no demonstration-copying cause or translation-quality certification is inferred from numerical errors alone.

All 380 records pass provenance and completeness checks; 285 offline tests pass. Main limitations are ten development questions, possible exposure to public training items, mixed-language units and model/runtime confounding. Gemma 3 4B provides the main selection comparison; other models are reference arms. Dataset improvement and renewed baseline analysis precede the planned novelty work.
